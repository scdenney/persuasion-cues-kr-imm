# Persuasion and Prejudice: Are South Korean Attitudes Toward Immigration Open to Change?

[Working paper](https://scdenney.github.io/research/) by **Steven Denney**, **Isa van Dam**, and **Christopher Green** (Leiden University).

## Overview

The paper asks whether pro-immigration framing cues the South Korean executive has used since 2018 — economic competitiveness and fertility-related demographic concerns — move public support for expanded immigration. We field a nationally representative survey experiment (n = 1,999, January 2024) that crosses three between-subjects framing arms (control, growth cue, birthrate cue) with a forced-choice conjoint of immigrant-admission profiles.

## Headline findings

The cues operate at two different levels.

- **Policy preferences polarize.** The growth cue consolidates the status quo among centrists and conservatives but pushes self-placed progressives toward restriction, more than doubling their share favoring lower immigration. The pattern replicates partially on partisanship.
- **Admission preferences are stable.** Origin, religion, language, co-ethnicity, and skill penalties and premiums in the conjoint hold at the magnitudes the cross-national literature documents, independent of the cue. An exploratory analysis identifies one corner (work history) where the fertility cue moves things at small magnitudes.

Together, the cues shift stated policy preferences through a partisan source-cue channel but leave the deeper hierarchies that organize preferences over which immigrants should be admitted in place.
